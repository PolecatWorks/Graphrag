# Apollo 12 and Pete Conrad

Apollo 12 (November 14–24, 1969) was the sixth crewed flight in the Apollo program and the second to land on the Moon. Launched during a rainstorm by a Saturn V rocket, the spacecraft was struck by lightning shortly after liftoff, but quick troubleshooting by the crew and flight controllers restored telemetry.

The mission was commanded by Charles "Pete" Conrad Jr. (June 2, 1930 – July 8, 1999), an American naval officer, aviator, aeronautical engineer, and NASA astronaut who previously flew on Gemini 5 and Gemini 11. Conrad became the third person to walk on the Moon. Stepping onto the surface from Lunar Module Intrepid, the diminutive Conrad famously exclaimed: "Whoopee! Man, that may have been a small one for Neil, but that's a long one for me!"

Pete Conrad and Lunar Module Pilot Alan Bean performed two extravehicular activities on the Ocean of Storms (Oceanus Procellarum), completing a precise pinpoint landing near the robotic Surveyor 3 spacecraft and retrieving parts of it for scientific analysis on Earth, while Command Module Pilot Richard F. Gordon Jr. operated in lunar orbit aboard the Command Module Yankee Clipper.
