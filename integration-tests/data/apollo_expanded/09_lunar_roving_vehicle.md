# Lunar Roving Vehicle

The Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV), popularly known as the Moon Buggy, was a battery-powered four-wheeled rover used on the Moon in the last three missions of the American Apollo program: Apollo 15, Apollo 16, and Apollo 17 during 1971 and 1972.

Manufactured by Boeing with Delco Electronics providing mobility subsystems, the LRV was transported to the Moon folded into a quadrant of the Apollo Lunar Module's descent stage. Once deployed on the lunar surface, it could carry two astronauts, their life support systems, cameras, scientific instruments, and collected lunar rock samples.

The LRV operated with four 0.25-horsepower electric motors, one on each wheel, and was powered by two 36-volt silver-zinc non-rechargeable batteries. It allowed astronauts to travel significant distances across the lunar landscape—up to 4.7 miles (7.6 km) away from the lunar module on Apollo 17—greatly expanding the scientific range and geological sample collection of the Apollo exploration missions.
