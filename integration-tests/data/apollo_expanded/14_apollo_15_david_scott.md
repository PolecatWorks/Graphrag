# Apollo 15: David Scott and Hadley Rille

Apollo 15 (July 26 – August 7, 1971) was the fourth mission to land humans on the Moon and the first of the extended "J-missions" designed for longer stays and enhanced surface science. The crew comprised Commander David Scott, Lunar Module Pilot James Irwin, and Command Module Pilot Alfred Worden.

Scott and Irwin landed Lunar Module Falcon in the dramatic Hadley-Apennine region, nestled between 15,000-foot lunar mountains and the deep volcanic canyon known as Hadley Rille. The mission was the first to deploy the Boeing-built Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV), allowing Scott and Irwin to drive 17.5 miles across the surface and discover the famous Genesis Rock, an anorthosite sample dating back over 4 billion years to the Moon's early crust.

Before leaving the Moon, David Scott conducted Galileo's famous physics demonstration by dropping a 2.9-pound aluminum geology hammer and a 0.06-pound falcon feather simultaneously, confirming that in a vacuum all objects fall at the same rate regardless of mass. Meanwhile, Alfred Worden orbited in Command Module Endeavour conducting orbital photography, later performing a deep-space spacewalk during the journey back to Earth to retrieve film cassettes.
