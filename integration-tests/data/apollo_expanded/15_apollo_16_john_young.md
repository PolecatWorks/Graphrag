# Apollo 16: John Young and Descartes Highlands

Apollo 16 (April 16–27, 1972) was the fifth lunar landing mission and the second J-mission in the Apollo program. The spacecraft was commanded by legendary astronaut John Young, with Charles Duke serving as Lunar Module Pilot and Ken Mattingly as Command Module Pilot.

Young and Duke piloted Lunar Module Orion to a precise landing in the Descartes Highlands, an area chosen to sample what geologists previously hypothesized were volcanic landforms. Upon exploration with their Lunar Roving Vehicle, Young and Duke discovered that the rocks were not volcanic but impact breccias created by massive meteorite bombardments, fundamentally reshaping scientific theories of lunar highland evolution.

During three moonwalks spanning over 20 hours, John Young famously executed a "salute jump" on the lunar surface, and Charles Duke left an aluminum-encased photograph of his family on the lunar soil. In lunar orbit aboard Command Module Casper, Ken Mattingly mapped the equatorial regions and conducted chemical spectroscopy before performing an extravehicular spacewalk during trans-Earth coast to retrieve exposed scientific film.
