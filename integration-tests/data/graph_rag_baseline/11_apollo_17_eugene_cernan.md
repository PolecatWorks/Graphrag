# Apollo 17 and Eugene Cernan

Apollo 17 (December 7–19, 1972) was the final crewed Moon landing mission of the Apollo program. It broke several records for crewed spaceflight, including the longest crewed lunar landing flight (301 hours and 51 minutes), longest total lunar surface extravehicular activities (22 hours and 4 minutes), largest lunar sample return (approximately 115 kg or 249 lb), and the longest time spent in lunar orbit (147 hours and 48 minutes).

The mission was commanded by Eugene "Gene" Cernan (March 14, 1934 – January 16, 2017), a naval aviator, electrical engineer, and astronaut who had previously flown on Gemini 9A and Apollo 10. Accompanied by lunar module pilot and geologist Harrison "Jack" Schmitt in the Lunar Module Challenger, Cernan conducted extensive surface expeditions in the Taurus-Littrow valley using the Lunar Roving Vehicle, while Ronald Evans remained in lunar orbit aboard the command module America.

As he re-entered the lunar module after the final moonwalk on December 14, 1972, Gene Cernan became the last person to walk on the surface of the Moon, speaking the words: "we leave as we came and, God willing, as we shall return, with peace and hope for all mankind."
