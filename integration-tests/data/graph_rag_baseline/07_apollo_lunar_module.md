# Apollo Lunar Module

The Apollo Lunar Module (LM), originally designated the Lunar Excursion Module (LEM), was the lander spacecraft that was flown from lunar orbit to the Moon's surface and back during the American Apollo program. Built by Grumman Aerospace under the direction of Thomas J. Kelly, the LM was the first crewed vehicle designed solely for operation in the vacuum of space.

The Lunar Module consisted of two distinct stages:
1. The Descent Stage: Carried the landing gear, descent propulsion engine, radar, surface exploration equipment, and on later missions, the Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV). It served as the launch pad when the crew departed from the lunar surface.
2. The Ascent Stage: Contained the crew cabin, environmental control life support systems, guidance and reaction control thrusters, and the ascent engine which lifted the two astronauts back into orbit to rendezvous and dock with the Command and Service Module (CSM).

The LM successfully carried astronauts down to the lunar surface across six missions: Apollo 11 (Eagle), Apollo 12 (Intrepid), Apollo 14 (Antares), Apollo 15 (Falcon), Apollo 16 (Orion), and Apollo 17 (Challenger), and served as a crucial lifeboat for the crew of Apollo 13 (Aquarius) following an in-flight explosion in their service module.
