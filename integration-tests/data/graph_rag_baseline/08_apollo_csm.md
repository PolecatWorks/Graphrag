# Apollo Command and Service Module

The Apollo Command and Service Module (CSM) was one of two principal components of the United States Apollo spacecraft, used for the Apollo program which landed astronauts on the Moon between 1969 and 1972. Built for NASA by North American Aviation, the CSM operated as a mother ship that carried three astronauts, the Lunar Module, and supplies between Earth and lunar orbit.

The spacecraft comprised two interconnected sections:
1. Command Module (CM): A conical habitable capsule that served as living quarters and control deck for the three crew members during launch, translunar cruise, lunar orbit, trans-Earth return, and atmospheric reentry. It was the only component of the spacecraft stack designed with a heat shield capable of surviving reentry to splash down safely in the ocean.
2. Service Module (SM): An unpressurized cylindrical structure that supported the Command Module with electrical power from fuel cells, reaction control system (RCS) propellant, high-gain communications antennas, environmental oxygen, water, and the Service Propulsion System (SPS) main engine which executed lunar orbit insertion and trans-Earth injection burns.

During landing missions, one astronaut (the Command Module Pilot, such as Michael Collins on Apollo 11) remained in lunar orbit aboard the CSM while the other two astronauts descended to the surface in the Lunar Module.
