# Saturn V Rocket

The Saturn V was an American human-rated super heavy-lift launch vehicle used by NASA between 1967 and 1973. The three-stage liquid-fueled launch vehicle was developed under the Apollo program for human exploration of the Moon, and was later used to launch Skylab, the first American space station.

Standing 363 feet (111 meters) tall and weighing 6.2 million pounds (2.8 million kg), the Saturn V remains one of the largest and most powerful rockets ever operated. Designed under the direction of Wernher von Braun and Arthur Rudolph at the Marshall Space Flight Center, it generated 7.5 million pounds-force (33,000 kN) of thrust at liftoff using five Rocketdyne F-1 engines on the S-IC first stage.

The second stage, S-II, was powered by five J-2 engines burning liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen. The third stage, S-IVB, used a single J-2 engine capable of restarting in orbit to execute the translunar injection (TLI) burn that sent the Apollo Command and Service Module (CSM) and Lunar Module (LM) toward the Moon.
