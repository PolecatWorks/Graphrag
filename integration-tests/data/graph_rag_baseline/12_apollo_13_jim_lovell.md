# Apollo 13: Jim Lovell, Odyssey, and Aquarius

Apollo 13 (April 11–17, 1970) was the seventh crewed mission in the Apollo space program, famously dubbed a "successful failure" after an in-flight explosion crippled the spacecraft en route to the Moon. The mission was commanded by veteran astronaut Jim Lovell, with Jack Swigert as Command Module Pilot and Fred Haise as Lunar Module Pilot.

Two days after launch, approximately 200,000 miles from Earth, an oxygen tank in the Service Module of Command/Service Module Odyssey exploded due to damaged electrical insulation. The explosion ruptured the oxygen system and disabled Odyssey's electrical fuel cells. The crew was forced to evacuate Odyssey and utilize the Lunar Module Aquarius as a cosmic lifeboat, conserving power, water, and oxygen while using the lunar gravitational field to execute a free-return trajectory around the far side of the Moon.

Through ingenious teamwork between the astronauts and flight controllers led by Gene Kranz in Houston, the crew modified Aquarius's lithium hydroxide canisters to purge carbon dioxide, survived near-freezing temperatures, and re-docked with Odyssey just prior to reentry, splashing down safely in the South Pacific Ocean.
