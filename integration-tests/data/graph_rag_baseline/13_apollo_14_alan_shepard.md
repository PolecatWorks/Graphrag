# Apollo 14: Alan Shepard and Edgar Mitchell

Apollo 14 (January 31 – February 9, 1971) was the eighth crewed mission in the Apollo program and the third to land on the Moon. It returned American astronauts to lunar exploration following the aborted Apollo 13 mission. 

The flight was commanded by Alan Shepard, who was the first American in space on Mercury-Redstone 3 (Freedom 7) in 1961. At age 47, Shepard became the oldest person to walk on the Moon and the only Mercury Seven astronaut to reach lunar orbit and land. Edgar Mitchell served as Lunar Module Pilot, and Stuart Roosa served as Command Module Pilot.

Shepard and Mitchell landed Lunar Module Antares in the Fra Mauro highlands, a region covered in ejecta blankets from the ancient Imbrium impact basin. During their two extravehicular activities, the duo collected 95 pounds of rock samples and Shepard famously rigged a six-iron golf club head to a sample tool handle, hitting two golf balls across the lunar surface. Stuart Roosa orbited above in Command Module Kitty Hawk, carrying hundreds of tree seeds that were later germinated across the United States as "Moon Trees".
